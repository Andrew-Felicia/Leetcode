The core idea behind solving the **Minimum Height Trees (MHTs)** problem comes down to one key observation:

> **The root of a Minimum Height Tree must be as close to the center of the tree as possible.**

If you pick a node near the outer edge (a leaf) as the root, the tree will be very tall and unbalanced. To make the tree as short as possible, you want to pick the node(s) sitting directly in the middle of the longest path in the tree.

---

### 🔑 Key Mathematical Insight: The Centroid(s)

In graph theory, a tree can have **at most 2 center nodes** (called centroids):

* If a tree has an **odd** diameter path length (e.g., $1 - 2 - 3$), there is **1** center node ($2$).
* If a tree has an **even** diameter path length (e.g., $1 - 2 - 3 - 4$), there are **2** center nodes ($2$ and $3$).

Our goal is simply to find these central nodes.

---

### ✂️ The Strategy: Topological Leaf Trimming (BFS Layer-by-Layer)

Instead of searching from the inside out (which is hard because we don't know where the center is), we **work from the outside in**. This is similar to peeling an onion layer by layer.

1. **Identify the Leaves:** Find all current leaf nodes. A leaf node is any node with **degree = 1** (it only connects to 1 neighbor).
2. **Trim the Leaves:** Remove all current leaf nodes from the tree and update their neighbors' connection counts.
3. **Repeat:** The neighbors that now have degree = 1 become the *new* outer layer of leaves. Remove them again.
4. **Stop Condition:** Keep trimming layer by layer until there are **$\le 2$ nodes remaining**. Those remaining 1 or 2 nodes are your MHT roots!

---

### 💡 Visual Walkthrough

Consider a tree line like this: `0 - 1 - 2 - 3 - 4` (5 total nodes)

```text
Step 0: Initial Tree
(0) --- (1) --- (2) --- (3) --- (4)
 ^                               ^
Leaf                            Leaf

Step 1: Trim leaves (0 and 4). Remaining nodes: 3.
        (1) --- (2) --- (3)
         ^               ^
     New Leaf        New Leaf

Step 2: Trim new leaves (1 and 3). Remaining nodes: 1.
                (2)
                 ^
             Centroid! (Stop, remaining <= 2)

```

The algorithm leaves us with `[2]`, which is the only MHT root for this tree.

---

### ⏱️ Why is this optimal?

* **Time Complexity:** $\mathcal{O}(n)$ — Every node and edge is inspected and removed at most once.
* **Space Complexity:** $\mathcal{O}(n)$ — We store the adjacency connections and a queue for the active leaves.
